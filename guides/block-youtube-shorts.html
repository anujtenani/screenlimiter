How to block YouTube Shorts on iPhone

Last updated November 2026

Shorts is a tab inside YouTube, not an app — so there is no switch that turns it off. There are still two ways to stop it eating the evening, and one of them is honest about what it costs.

The honest answer first. There is no setting on iOS, and no third-party app, that blocks YouTube Shorts while leaving the rest of YouTube available. Shorts is a tab inside the YouTube app, and Apple's Screen Time framework can only see whole applications, categories and web domains. Anything claiming otherwise is either blocking all of YouTube or doing nothing.

Why Shorts cannot be blocked on its own

Screen Time gives an app a token for an application, a category or a web domain. It does not expose the internal navigation of another company's app — not its tabs, not its sub-feeds, not its in-app browser history. YouTube has no API that lets another app reach in and hide a tab, and there is no setting inside YouTube that turns Shorts off.

This is not a limitation Screen Limiter is working around. It is the boundary of the platform, and every app in this category lives inside it.

Option 1: a per-hour allowance on YouTube

This is the approach that works, and it is blunt on purpose: you are limiting YouTube, which happens to limit Shorts.

  1. Open Screen Limiter and tap +.
  2. Under apps, choose YouTube. Leave categories alone.
  3. Set the allowance to 5 minutes, the window to 1 hour, and the strictness to Normal.
  4. Save. The first window starts at the next boundary.

From then on, YouTube works for five minutes an hour and then gets a shield that says when the next five arrive. The Shorts tab is in that budget along with everything else.

If you actually need YouTube for work

Five minutes an hour is the right number for the habit and the wrong number for a tutorial. Two workable shapes:

  • Split by time of day. One group restricted to working hours with 15 minutes an hour, another for the evening with 5. Groups can be restricted to a set of hours, so the same app can have a generous budget at 2pm and a mean one at 10pm.
  • Keep the app, move the habit. Watch the tutorial on a laptop, where the recommendation engine has less purchase on you, and leave the phone's YouTube on five minutes.

Option 2: Apple's daily App Limit

Settings → Screen Time → App Limits → Add Limit, choose YouTube, set a daily time, save. It is already installed and it is free.

Two things make it leak. The budget is daily, so it can be spent by mid-morning and then you are either locked out for the rest of the day or pressing the bypass — and the bypass is right there. Turning on Block at End of Limit removes the bypass, which helps, but then a single morning's Shorts session can cost you the whole day.

Option 3: the web version, with a content blocker

If you are willing to give up the app, Safari plus a content blocker that can hide the Shorts shelf is a genuine option. It is fiddly — the shelf moves, the markup changes, and YouTube experiments constantly — and it does not stop the app being one tap away if it is still installed. As a way of making the browser version tolerable it works; as a block it does not.

What does not work

  • A Screen Time website limit. Limiting youtube.com does not limit the YouTube app. They are different tokens.
  • Focus modes. A Focus can silence notifications. It does not hide a tab or block an app.
  • Restricted Mode. It filters content, not surfaces. Shorts is not unfiltered content; it is a layout.
  • Deleting the app and using the web. This works until you reinstall the app, which takes about forty seconds.

The setup worth doing once

If Shorts is the specific thing that disappears your evening, the combination that holds up over a month is:

  1. YouTube on a 5-minute, hourly allowance, Normal mode, so the shield is firm but the escape is not a wall.
  2. A second YouTube group for the two hours before bed, with a smaller allowance and Hard mode.
  3. Shorts removed from the Home Screen. Not deleted — just not on the first screen.

The third sounds like the weakest of the three and does more than it has any right to. The difference between an app you have to search for and an app you tap without looking is the difference between a decision and a reflex.

Questions

Can I block Shorts only, and keep normal YouTube?

Not with any app on the App Store. iOS identifies whole applications and web domains, not tabs. Screen Limiter limits YouTube as an application, which includes Shorts.

Does the YouTube app have a setting to hide Shorts?

No. Shorts is a core surface for YouTube and there is no official switch to remove it. Any guide offering one is describing a content filter or a web-only workaround.

Will blocking YouTube stop Shorts appearing in search?

No. Shorts results inside search are part of the same app and the same session, and they are affected by the same allowance rather than by anything Shorts-specific.

Can Screen Limiter block YouTube in Safari but not the app?

You can limit youtube.com as a web domain and leave the app alone, or the reverse. They are separate selections in the picker, and most people who want the block want both.
