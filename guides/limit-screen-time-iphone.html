How to set app limits on iPhone

Last updated November 2026

Apple's own limits take two minutes to set up and are free. They also leak in three predictable places — here is the setup, and then the diagnosis.

Apple's Screen Time limits, step by step

App Limits

  1. Open Settings → Screen Time.
  2. Tap App Limits → Add Limit.
  3. Choose a category, or tap the disclosure to pick individual apps. One limit can cover several apps and categories.
  4. Set the time, then tap Customize Days if weekends should differ — you can set a different budget per weekday.
  5. Turn on Block at End of Limit if you want the block to be final rather than offering the bypass. Save, then tap Add.

Downtime

  1. Settings → Screen Time → Downtime.
  2. Set a schedule. Everything is blocked during it except the apps on the Always Allowed list.
  3. Add the calls, messages and maps you genuinely need to Always Allowed.

Make the limits harder to remove

  1. Settings → Screen Time, scroll to Lock Screen Time Settings.
  2. Set a Screen Time passcode that somebody else knows, or that you will not find convenient at 11pm.

Without a passcode, every limit on this page is a suggestion with a settings screen one tap away.

Why they leak

The bypass is printed on the block

When an App Limit runs out, iOS offers "Ignore Limit" with options to extend. Each extension teaches you that the limit is negotiable, and a negotiable limit is decoration within a week.

The budget is daily

One daily allowance can be spent at 8am and is then gone. By the time you need the app, the only routes are bypassing or waiting until midnight, and both are worse than the problem.

One limit covers too much or too little

A limit on "Social" catches apps you do not care about, so you raise it for the one you do. A per-app limit is better, and Apple makes you set one per app.

Nothing enforces the reset

Apple's limits are daily, and a daily limit is a wall with a clock on it. The hours you wanted to protect are not the hours the budget knows about.

The usual bypasses

  • Changing the device clock or time zone, which is widely reported to reset daily limits.
  • Deleting and reinstalling the app you limited, which removes its limit with it.
  • Using the same app on a second device, or the web version, which the limit does not cover.
  • Asking someone else to "just check something" on your phone.

When Apple's limits are the right answer

If you want one ceiling for the day, per app, with the least setup, Apple's limits are already on your phone and they are free. They are also the correct tool for a child's device, where the passcode, the family sharing and the ability to change things remotely matter more than the shape of the allowance.

When a repeating window is the right answer

If your problem is specific sessions — the evening, the morning, the bit after work — a daily budget is the wrong instrument. A small allowance that returns every hour keeps the app usable all day and stops the session that was going to cost you two of them.

Screen Limiter does that: +, pick the apps, 5–30 minutes per group, a 30, 60 or 120-minute window, and a strictness level per group. It cannot block a tab inside another app, and it is not a parental-control product — but it is the shape of limit that survives a bad day, which is the only kind that matters.

Questions

What is the difference between App Limits and Downtime?

App Limits caps total time per app or category per day. Downtime blocks everything except an allowlist during set hours. Downtime is a boundary; App Limits is a budget.

Can I set different limits for weekends?

Yes. When creating an App Limit, tap Customize Days and give each weekday its own time. The limit still resets daily.

Why did my App Limit stop working?

The common causes are the Screen Time passcode being turned off, the app being reinstalled, the limit being edited, or the device clock being changed. Screen Time also quietly stops enforcing some limits after a software update until the limit is re-saved.

Do I need a Screen Time passcode?

If the limit is for someone else, yes. If it is for you, it is the difference between a boundary and a note to self. Set one you will not have at 11pm.
