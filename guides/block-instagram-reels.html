How to block Instagram Reels on iPhone

Last updated November 2026

Reels cannot be switched off, so the block has to go around the whole app. That trade-off is the entire design problem, and it is solvable if you are honest about which half of Instagram you actually need.

Instagram has no off switch for Reels. There is no setting in the app that removes the tab, and iOS cannot see inside it. Every real solution either limits Instagram as a whole or changes what the app is allowed to do at certain times.

What Instagram will and will not do for you

Instagram's Your activity screen offers a daily reminder — you choose a time, it tells you when you have reached it. That is a notification, not a block: it fires while you are mid-scroll and asks you to be reasonable, which is the weakest possible moment to ask. Its "time spent" panel shows you the number, and the number is not a lever.

Reels recommendations are driven by watch history rather than follows, so unfollowing people does not empty the tab. Marking things "Not interested" helps the recommendations and does nothing about the habit.

Option 1: limit Instagram by the hour

The most durable approach, because it changes the shape of the session instead of relying on you to end it.

  1. In Screen Limiter, tap + and pick Instagram.
  2. Allowance: 5 minutes. Window: 1 hour. Strictness: Normal.
  3. Save.

Five minutes an hour is enough to read a message, reply, and check that nobody has died. It is not enough to fall into Reels. When the minutes are gone the shield names the time the next five arrive, which makes closing the app a decision with an end rather than a loss.

If you need DMs during the day

Ten minutes an hour is a fair compromise: enough for a conversation, not enough for a scroll. The alternative — leaving Instagram unlimited and hoping — is the configuration that was already not working.

Option 2: Apple's daily limit on Instagram

Settings → Screen Time → App Limits → Add Limit, choose Instagram, set a daily time. Free, built in, and fine for a first attempt.

The weakness is the daily budget: a slow morning spends it, and the rest of the day is either blocked or bypassed. For an app whose whole value is a two-minute message, "blocked until midnight" is worse than no limit at all, because it teaches you to press the bypass.

Option 3: Downtime, for a hard evening boundary

Settings → Screen Time → Downtime can block everything except an allowlist between set hours. It is a sledgehammer and occasionally the right one — a 22:00–07:00 window where Instagram is simply not available is a clean boundary that requires no discipline at the moment it matters.

What does not work

  • A Focus mode. Silences notifications. Does not hide Reels.
  • Restricting the app's content. Reels still loads.
  • Unfollowing. Recommendations do not depend on follows.
  • Deleting it. Genuinely effective, and most people reinstall within a fortnight — usually for a message they needed to read.

The arrangement that tends to stick

Instagram is not one thing; it is a messaging app with a slot machine attached. Treat those as two jobs:

  • Messaging should be easy and quick. A generous allowance (10–15 minutes an hour) keeps it usable without inviting a session.
  • Reels should be expensive. The way to make it expensive without breaking messaging is strictness: Hard mode on the group, so a deliberate decision is needed and there is one of them a day.
  • Evening should be stricter than the afternoon. A second group restricted to the two hours before bed, with five minutes and a 30-minute window, is the smallest change with the largest effect.

Questions

Can I block Reels without blocking Instagram?

No. Reels is a surface inside the Instagram app and iOS does not expose it. Blocking Reels means blocking Instagram for that window.

Does Instagram's own daily reminder work?

As a nudge for someone already paying attention, yes. As a limit, no — it is a notification you can dismiss while continuing to scroll, which is the moment it is least likely to be obeyed.

Will limiting Instagram break my messages?

While the shield is up, the app is blocked, messages included. That is why the allowance matters: ten minutes an hour is enough to keep up with a conversation and small enough that Reels never gets a proper run.

Is there a Reels-specific app for iPhone?

Not one that works the way you want. Any app that claims to block one tab inside another company's app is either using a web wrapper or overstating what it does.
