How to limit Instagram to 5 minutes per hour on iPhone

Last updated November 2026

The short answer: Apple's built-in Screen Time cannot do this. Its App Limits are daily caps. An allowance that resets every hour needs an app built for that, and there are two on the App Store — Precursor and Screen Limiter. Here is both halves, including what neither can do.

What Apple Screen Time can do

Screen Time is free, built in, and worth understanding before you install anything.

  • App Limits put a daily cap on an app or a category — five minutes of Instagram, say. But that is five minutes per day, and you can only vary it by weekday, never by hour.
  • Downtime schedules quiet hours. It is a schedule, not a budget: apps are either allowed or not during the window.
  • A Screen Time passcode stops the limits being edited in a weak moment — genuinely useful, and free.

What it cannot do

There is no setting that makes an App Limit reset hourly, and no Shortcuts automation that meters how many minutes you used inside each hour — a timer can toggle a limit, but it cannot count usage, which is the whole point of an allowance. If you find a workaround online, test it on a spare afternoon before trusting it.

Doing it in Screen Limiter

  1. Install Screen Limiter and allow Screen Time access when iOS asks. There is no account.
  2. Tap + and choose Instagram in Apple's app picker. Add TikTok or anything else the habit migrates to — a group shares one allowance, so the habit cannot simply move next door.
  3. Set the allowance to 5 minutes.
  4. Set the window to 1 hour. (Screen Limiter also offers 30 minutes and two hours, per group.)
  5. Pick a strictness — Easy, Normal or Hard — and save.

From then on you have five minutes of Instagram each hour. Spend them however you like: checking a message costs thirty seconds, a scroll costs the rest. When they are gone the app is shielded, the shield names the time the minutes return, and at the next hour boundary the block lifts by itself — no app to open, nothing to remember.

Precursor does this too, and did it first. Its allowance is per hour with three strictness levels, a passcode and Parent Mode. If you want the classic hourly shape, it is a fair choice — the head-to-head says exactly where each app is better.

The fine print

  • The window is aligned to the clock. With a one-hour window, minutes spent at 1:55 come back at 2:00, not at 2:55. A 30-minute window turns at :00 and :30; a two-hour window on the even hour.
  • Five minutes is the floor. iOS measures app usage coarsely, so smaller allowances would re-block almost immediately and feel broken rather than firm.
  • Background audio and video count. Apple's usage accounting includes background playback and no third-party app can opt out. A video playing in the background spends the allowance.
  • Deleting the app removes every limit. The optional passcode protects your settings from an impulsive edit; it is not a lock on your own phone.
  • Transitions land within about a minute of the boundary, because iOS decides when the window turns.

Why five minutes an hour beats sixty a day

The arithmetic is nearly the same; the shape is not. Sixty minutes in one daily budget tends to disappear in one or two long sits before lunch, and then the limit punishes you for the rest of the day. Five minutes that return every hour mean you can always check in soon, so putting the phone down is easier and the long sit never gets room to build.

Most windows will pass untouched. That is the point — the allowance is there so that leaving costs nothing, not so that you spend it.

FAQ

Can Apple Screen Time limit an app per hour?

No. App Limits are daily caps, adjustable per weekday but never per hour, and there is no built-in way to make one reset on the hour.

Is there an app that gives you 5 minutes of Instagram per hour?

Yes — Precursor (hourly) and Screen Limiter (30, 60 or 120-minute windows). Both use Apple's Screen Time frameworks, so the block is a real system shield rather than an overlay.

Does the allowance reset at the top of the hour, or an hour after I start?

At the clock boundary. Windows are aligned to midnight, so a one-hour window turns at :00, a 30-minute window at :00 and :30. The shield always names the exact time your minutes return.

What happens when the five minutes run out?

Instagram is shielded until the window turns. On Easy you can wave the block away from the shield; on Normal five stops a day require a short hold in the app; on Hard you get one stop a day with an eight-second hold.

Can I put Instagram and TikTok under one hourly limit?

Yes. A group can hold several apps, categories or websites and they share one allowance, so the habit cannot simply move to the next app.

Does it work if Screen Limiter is closed?

Yes. The next window is scheduled with iOS ahead of time, so the block lifts at the boundary whether the app is open, in the background, or force-quit.
